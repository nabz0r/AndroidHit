# UX rationale for 1.12

Research checked on 28 October 2026. These references inform implementation choices; they are not a measured retention study of Luma Drift. The prior 1.11 tests established bounded functional behavior, not visual appeal, comprehension, or enjoyment.

## Concrete problems in the 1.11 source

- The common wallet shows only earned LumaCoins. LumaGold appears as a shop category, so users cannot reliably distinguish a balance from a destination or a missing connection.
- Item browsing, purchasing, pinning and equipping repeatedly open system alert dialogs, removing the player from the canvas composition. A clothing purchase immediately opens another offer. These interruptions are technically functional but fragment the flow.
- Shop powers use almost identical rectangular rows, with an icon, stock and pack price. The effect is hidden in a separate offer. A player must remember the effect while comparing stock and price across screens.
- A saved cosmetic identity and a visible result are distinct requirements. The 1.11 real-raster and cold-start tests should be retained in the new presentation.

## Design decisions and their evidence

1. **Make money explicit.** Give each currency a stable icon shape, its complete name and its own balance or honest unavailable state. Do not use only yellow versus purple to encode meaning. Android's current guidance requires labels that explain purpose and cues other than color. A premium balance that cannot be verified should not be displayed as a confirmed zero. [Android accessibility principles](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/principles).
2. **Keep browsing in context.** Item details become a normal navigable canvas page, with a visible Close action returning to the caller. Successful equip and earned purchases use brief nonblocking feedback. Material describes snackbars as short process feedback that should not interrupt the user's experience. This supports reducing modal interruptions; it does not mean replacing every confirmation indiscriminately. [Material 3 snackbar](https://m3.material.io/components/snackbar/overview), [official Android component guidance](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/components/Snackbar.md).
3. **Show effect, stock and price together.** A distinct illustration and short explanation lets the player recognize the tool rather than recall information from a dismissed popup. Keep touch targets at least 48 × 48 dp and avoid overlapping expanded targets. This is a usability hypothesis for this game that still requires human observation. [Android touch target guidance](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858?hl=en), [Android Views accessibility](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/views/apps-views).
4. **Support competence and choice.** Show the actual effect of equip, explain why an item is locked, and retain player-selected goals with honest progress. Research on self-determination in games links competence, autonomy and relatedness with enjoyment and preference. It does not establish that more rewards, more pressure or more screens necessarily improve engagement. [Przybylski, Rigby and Ryan, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0019440), [author-hosted paper](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2010_PrzybylskiRigbyRyan_ROGP.pdf).
5. **Use motion as information.** Purchase/equip feedback and a small character reaction can acknowledge success. Decorative elements must remain outside the accessibility traversal, and meaningful state must remain available without animation or sound. Preserve reduced-motion behavior. [Android accessibility principles](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/principles).

The scope does not introduce manufactured scarcity, misleading near misses, streak-loss threats, opaque currency conversion or pressure based on money already spent. Engaging play is an implementation objective, not a claim that the product is a hit.

## New validation, kept separate from history

The copied 1.11 SDK runner and fixture inventory are recorded in [the 1.12 provenance file](../../scripts/v112-ui-test/provenance-v111.json). New test scripts and output paths must remain separate from `v111-ui-test` and `docs/validation/v1.11`.

Acceptance should cover both named currencies and honest account state; category selection; actual touch bounds; browsing and Close without an economic mutation; earned purchases with the exact debit; explicit Equip with visible raster change; persistence through a real process restart; all six translations; and reduced-motion use. Provider controls, real ads, real-money payments and the public site remain outside this UI exercise. Native evidence establishes the tested behavior only. A human test is still needed to assess whether players understand the layout and find the items appealing.
