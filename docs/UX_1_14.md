# Puzzle clarity — 1.14

The core problem is a mismatch between the rule the game executes and the rule a new player can infer. In historical L15, a left-moving switch is waiting behind a closed left side; exiting it closes the upper side. Two manual openings are necessary. The former screen showed a small lock and a general objective, so a clear-looking trajectory could still be refused and consume the move allowance.

The revised main route teaches one layer at a time:

| Journey boards | Teaching focus |
| --- | --- |
| N1–4 | Straight exits and the large direction arrow |
| N5–8 | Present pieces blocking a row or column |
| N9–12 | A K piece leaves to dissolve its matching seals |
| N13–16 | A closed side is a button; opening costs one action |
| N17–20 | The small switch arrow names a gate changed after exit |
| N21–24 | The order of two switches changes the gate state |
| N25–27 | Keys combined with switches |
| N28–30 | Controlled combinations of the introduced rules |

The large arrow gives movement. Small arrows name gate effects. K numbers associate a key with seals and do not prescribe numerical order. A side gate controls every lane on that side. These distinctions are stated in the board’s contextual help and the prelevel explanation.

Closed gates use a visibly separate control, cost mark and confirmation preview. Its drawn rectangle and 50×50 target stay outside the cells. Piece tap bounds, drag ownership and trajectory rays use the same board dimensions. A preview does not spend an action; confirmation does. The low-budget warning appears before exhaustion.

The budget stays consequential. Productive exits and gate openings cost one; blocked tries are free. One Return restores the preceding position and refunds its action. The quota is saved with the active run and does not refresh after a process restart. A free restart begins a new attempt. The failure screen offers those concrete choices rather than a new paid continuation under this policy.

Causal feedback observes the actual board: the changed gate mask, the nearest blocker or seal, and the set of exits newly made legal. A highlight explains what changed; it does not assert that the next move is globally optimal. Spark and the other retained aids have their explicit inventory limits. The guide itself never invokes a solver or spends an item.

No Hard/Expert run exceeds four boards. The score and class of every board are recalculated after its initial gates are authored; the 200-board median scores rise while individual boards retain recovery dips. The progression is authored and structurally audited. Those checks do not predict human pass rate, perceived difficulty or replay. The next product evidence is whether first-time players can explain a closed side, the two arrow sizes, a K pair and a costly gate order while playing the real candidate.

The final native image review also retains two minor copy/timing observations: the English one-piece message uses a plural, and the brief causal notice can remain visible during a very rapid transition into the next board. The unedited English N15 capture shows this transient notice; the budget and controls match the new board. These are remaining polish items, separate from solvability and action accounting.
