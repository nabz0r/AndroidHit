# Luma Drift 1.6 — UX candidate

**Internal release candidate · 27 October 2026 · installed emulator checks recorded below.**

This revision makes the next action, item ownership and puzzle rules more explicit through new menus, saga pages, prelevel information and original background art. The final Lab and production candidates were installed and exercised on the identified emulator. A source implementation, automated tap or screenshot alone does not establish physical-phone readability or complete accessibility. [1.6 qualification record](VALIDATION_1_6.md), [evidence directory](validation/v1.6/). Earlier [1.5 evidence](VALIDATION.md) retains its original scope.

## Problem and design decisions

The previous Canvas renderer worked in a 390-unit logical width. Its `fit()` method reduced a requested text size in proportion to measured overflow, without a lower size bound. Several collection, navigation and source captions requested only 9–12 logical units before that reduction. Translated names and purchase conditions could therefore fit inside a card while becoming difficult to read. Logical Canvas units are not Android `sp` or `dp`; a screenshot's pixel size does not establish physical readability.

The candidate changes the layout before shortening a rule: fewer simultaneous cards, stronger hierarchy, explicit status labels and separate details. Essential text includes the next action, price, quantity, ownership, unlock requirement, move budget and confirmation consequence. It should wrap or receive more space rather than silently shrink to fit. Cosmetic decoration and background art should leave these areas quiet.

| Area | Previous presentation | Candidate 1.6 direction | Acceptance still required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Navigation | Five compact destinations and many secondary entrances | Five pinned destinations: Home / Map / Events / Inventory / Shop, with shorter labels and consistent routes | Back navigation, focus order, current-tab state and usable hit areas |
| Journey / Remix | Thirty-node chapter grid | Ten nodes per saga page, a visible current node and chapter navigation | First/last pages, unlock boundaries, no node beyond 1000/240 |
| Inventory | Two narrow item columns and separate power shop | Three wider cards per page; Companions / Themes / Equipment / Powers tabs, All / Owned / To unlock filters | Long names, price/quantity visibility, empty filters, selection updates |
| Before a level | Rule and difficulty information spread across screens | Goal, actual mechanic, authored difficulty and budget before Play | Facts match the selected board; cancel does not start or spend |
| Profile | Cached connected/guest presentation | A single explicit session snapshot with refreshing/error/reconnect states | Real provider and foreground/recreation tests; no stale account display |
| Atmosphere | Code-drawn gradients and particles | Original generated background under readable UI surfaces | Asset provenance, device decode/memory cost and installed contrast |

These describe the current candidate, including the lobby profile/next-level card, linked inventory/shop entrances and a prelevel Powers shortcut. Installed menu/navigation checks now exist for the exact hashes below. Remaining acceptance work includes physical-phone use, a complete contrast/target audit and human playtesting.

### Physical layout and text policy

The [pure layout](../app/src/main/java/com/androidhit/lumadrift/ui/ExperienceLayout.java) uses width and density for scale, then scrolls when the safe viewport is shorter than its 840-unit content. It excludes top/bottom system insets and centers content on wide windows, with a maximum enlargement of 1.25 times density. Height no longer forces every label and button to shrink. Navigation is drawn separately at the viewport bottom while menu content scrolls above it.

The first candidate introduced a **12-logical-unit** text floor. That alone would render at about 9.85 dp on a 320 dp-wide window. The current layout instead supplies `minimumTextSize`, converting a **12 dp physical floor** back to logical units. Text/fit/wrap helpers use that floor. A preferred font scale raises the floor through **1.3**, reaching 15.6 dp; lower preferences do not lower the 12 dp minimum. Greater preferences remain capped. This applies to the minimum, not proportional scaling of every heading; it is not complete Android `sp` handling or adaptive 200% text support. Overflowing single-line captions can be ellipsized, so details must still expose the complete rule/price before confirmation.

Text size does not enlarge a hit rectangle or a puzzle cell. An eight-cell board occupying 300 logical units has 37.5-unit cells: about 30.77 dp at a 320 dp window width. Some 45-unit controls are also below the 48 dp target recommendation at that width. The floor and larger cards improve the design's bounds, but do not establish universal target-size compliance. Installed gesture, clipping, font-scale and accessibility review remains necessary.

## Primary references and their scope

King's official Candy Crush help describes a goal screen before Play, a level-type explanation and warnings before harder levels. Luma Drift borrows the information order: tell the player what to achieve and what changes on this board before asking them to begin. It uses its own rules, visual assets and copy. The reference does not establish that copying a presentation pattern improves retention. [King: level modes and goals](https://candycrush.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000754897-Which-game-modes-will-I-find).

Android recommends at least 48 × 48 dp interactive targets. Its text guidance uses at least 4.5:1 contrast for smaller text and 3:1 for qualifying larger text. These are acceptance references for the final physical rendering, including the image and any translucent layer behind a label. Canvas hit rectangles must be measured after transformation into screen coordinates and density; a 48-unit logical rectangle is not automatically 48 dp. [Android accessibility guidance](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/apps).

Android's Google Sign-In guidance supports a persistent sign-in button so a dismissed provider surface can be reopened. Returning from a provider sheet must not produce a dead button or a false connected state. Lifecycle callbacks also distinguish leaving, returning and process recreation. [Google Sign-In UI guidance](https://developer.android.com/identity/sign-in/credential-manager-siwg), [Activity lifecycle](https://developer.android.com/guide/components/activities/activity-lifecycle).

Firebase documents that a cached non-null user may have an invalid token, for example after remote deletion. This motivates explicit refresh/error states rather than equating a stored profile with a verified current session. [Firebase user management](https://firebase.google.com/docs/auth/android/manage-users).

References were checked on 27 October 2026. The design decisions above are project inferences from these sources, not statements endorsed by King or Google.

## Inventory: possession, source and effects

The [read-only presentation model](../app/src/main/java/com/androidhit/lumadrift/data/InventoryPresentation.java) builds immutable snapshots from existing public store APIs. It does not unlock items, equip them, write preferences, charge coins or consume powers. Refresh or claim actions remain in the application controller.

| State | Meaning | Label/action rule |
|---|---|---|
| Selected | The owned companion or theme is in use | Show **Selected**; do not present another purchase |
| Equipped | The owned equipment occupies its matching slot | Show **Equipped**, slot and stated benefit |
| Owned | The item is possessed but is not in use | Offer Select/Equip as appropriate |
| To unlock | The item is not possessed | Show its actual condition or earned-coin shop price |
| Ready to claim | A deterministic gear milestone is reached, but ownership is not yet recorded | A claim action may obtain it; reading a card never performs that action |

Selection never manufactures ownership. Companion/theme ownership includes the store's external account-ownership provider. Revoking that ownership or switching accounts removes an unsupported selection and exposes the store's valid fallback. Local earned ownership remains local. A displayed catalog condition describes how an unowned item can be obtained; it is not a claim about which currency an existing account-owned cosmetic originally used.

The catalogs remain **24 companions, six themes, 18 equipment items and six powers**. Equipment still has three slots: Headwear, Aura and Charm, six items each. The candidate changes presentation without changing prices or persistence. [Equipment contract](AVATAR_EQUIPMENT.md).

Source captions keep the unit visible:

- Journey: distinct Journey board clears, capped at the item's displayed threshold.
- Weekly: lifetime weekly board wins, including recorded earlier periods; not just the current seven-board event.
- Events: lifetime daily and weekly stars, including recorded earlier periods.
- Shop: the exact price in device-earned coins. This is separate from account purchased gold.
- Starter: available from the beginning.

Each equipment card should show its slot and concise effect. Detailed previews retain the full existing rules: Headwear pays only the bounded first-clear bonus; Aura reduces eligible earned-coin power-pack prices with a minimum price of one coin; Charm protects at most one blocked attempt per run. Owning several items does not stack their effects. Companions and themes change appearance only.

Power cards distinguish **Active**, **Ready**, **No charges** and **Locked**. Actual run state supplies Active; stock alone cannot infer an effect. An effect may remain active after its last charge was consumed. Locked saved stock does not bypass a Journey unlock milestone. Available stock is the store's count after reservation, and the displayed pack price comes from the store's current economy/Aura calculation. Details and confirmations must identify a one-charge activation without a second debit while active. The existing [durable power rules](MOVE_BUDGET_POWERUPS.md) remain authoritative.

`ExperienceStrings` provides short copy in **English, French, German, Spanish, Portuguese and Italian**. Its engine aliases cover `difficulty_*`, `beat_*`, `objective_*` and `element_*`; clear-board goals receive the board's actual piece count. Detailed rules remain in their existing translated catalogs. [Copy source](../app/src/main/java/com/androidhit/lumadrift/data/ExperienceStrings.java).

## Saga and prelevel information

`InventoryPresentation.sagaPage(total, pageIndex)` uses zero-based pages and one-based level numbers. Journey has 100 pages; its last page is **991–1000**. Remix has 24 pages; its last page is **231–240**. Empty or out-of-range requests cannot create a level zero or a board after the catalog end. Pagination changes presentation, not a saved player's earned stars.

The new prelevel panel should separate four questions:

1. **Goal:** release all pieces, or release matching keys to open seals before clearing the board.
2. **Mechanic:** arrows, gates, dual-gate switches, or keys and seals, derived from actual board metadata.
3. **Challenge:** Normal / Hard / Expert, an authored category rather than a measured success rate.
4. **Budget:** the board's actual piece count plus its fixed permitted margin; show the operative action limit without implying unlimited trial and error.

The candidate progression uses versioned, frozen J/K layouts and `ProgressionInfo`. Its Introduction / Practice / Peak / Recovery beats give the saga a readable rhythm. Keys and walls enter progressively rather than being restricted to the separate six P prototypes. The separate P route retains its own rules; the existence of normal-route keys does not silently give P rewards or powers. [Metadata source](../app/src/main/java/com/androidhit/lumadrift/engine/ProgressionInfo.java), [catalog routing](../app/src/main/java/com/androidhit/lumadrift/engine/BalancedCatalog.java).

The candidate introduces Journey keys at levels 12, 14 and 17, then at 22 and every six levels thereafter. Remix introduces them at 9, then 17 and every eight levels thereafter. The frozen selection contains 167 Journey key boards and 29 Remix key boards. These are the current source schedule and model audit, not installed-play evidence. [Exact audit and model limits](data/progression-v3-summary.md), [board-level audit](data/progression-v3-audit.csv).

The frozen engine's [progression report](validation/v1.6/progression-tests.txt) records **582,831 passing checks** across 1,240 new boards, including certified completion, an independent exact oracle, seal behavior, uniqueness, historical sessions/codes and fixed budgets. The first six Journey/Remix maps retain their earlier layouts. Journey 12 is an isolated key lesson with nine pieces, no switches and all gates open; 14 and 17 are authored Normal / Practice. [Offline regeneration](validation/v1.6/progression-reproduction.txt) reproduces the layout source byte for byte, SHA-256 `55aa4100e84365f12405b6633aaa928c37a5b5acb47afecfd877e8835d6b437b`. Legacy challenge families and already-started sessions retain their explicitly versioned paths. A new map label must not relabel an old saved board as a new layout or replace its budget mid-run. [Before](validation/v1.6/legacy-content-before.sha256) and [after](validation/v1.6/legacy-content-after.sha256) fingerprints agree for all eight historical families. [Detailed progression design](PROGRESSION_V3.md).

Solver success, number of valid choices and a random-choice policy can detect broken or trivial maps and compare fixed layouts. They do not model human learning, frustration, perceived fairness or enjoyment. Normal / Hard / Expert and the beat names therefore remain authored UX labels until human playtests calibrate them.

## Profile, refresh and lifecycle

The identity candidate exposes an immutable `SessionSnapshot` with **Guest, Refreshing, Connected, Reauthentication required and Error** states. Only a connected snapshot exposes the current profile. Refreshing, signing out, errors and account changes must not show the previous user's name as connected. The controller checks request generation and the expected UID before accepting asynchronous results. [Snapshot](../app/src/main/java/com/androidhit/lumadrift/identity/SessionSnapshot.java), [transition guard](../app/src/main/java/com/androidhit/lumadrift/identity/SessionTracker.java).

Foreground refresh is silent: reload the existing Google user and obtain a token, then update the snapshot. It does not open an account chooser without the player's sign-in action. Backgrounding defers network work as appropriate; a provider sheet can itself pause the host Activity, so its result must not be discarded solely because a pause occurred. Closing the controller invalidates late callbacks. [Controller](../app/src/main/java/com/androidhit/lumadrift/identity/GoogleAccountController.java).

The visible profile also states the storage boundary: ordinary gameplay/equipment progress is saved on this device; signing into Google is not a promise of cloud save. Purchased-gold account ownership and recovery remain a separate server contract. A disabled provider, unavailable network, cancelled chooser or required reauthentication needs an actionable state, with no false success toast. A known reauthentication requirement is persisted across controller recreation and other account screens; the wallet rejects that identity until it is resolved. The native Google button keeps its standard provider text, while the nearby status explains the need to reconnect.

Provider configuration, model tests and a mocked callback are insufficient to qualify a real Google login. Installed tests must cover a genuine sign-in, cancellation, return from the provider sheet, background/foreground, recreation, sign-out and stale/invalid-session handling with the exact signed candidate. No real login is claimed by this guide.

## Art and visual feedback

The requested background is original ImageGen artwork in the existing night-indigo, mint, peach and lilac family. It is decorative: it must not carry a puzzle arrow, goal, price or button label. Foreground panels keep a consistent reading surface; decorative stars, light and scenery should avoid the board and primary action. The [shared design system](DESIGN_SYSTEM.md) remains the reference for color, original assets and screenshot labeling.

The current packaged source is [aurora_atlas_v1.png](../app/src/main/res/drawable-nodpi/aurora_atlas_v1.png). [WorldBackdrop](../app/src/main/java/com/androidhit/lumadrift/ui/WorldBackdrop.java) decodes it once, requests RGB_565, reuses the bitmap and draw bounds, and applies a quieter layer behind play/inventory/shop/settings. The final installed menu captures show its rendering. A requested decode format is not a measured allocation or a physical-device startup/memory benchmark. Asset provenance and physical performance remain separate acceptance work. Do not identify promotional art as a gameplay screenshot.

Feedback should accompany changes in state: a selected/equipped badge updates immediately after successful persistence; a blocked purchase explains the missing requirement; a power becomes Active only after its effect starts; an exhausted or active power cannot look like a fresh purchase. Sound and color can reinforce these changes, but text, shape or an outline must also express them. Reduced motion should preserve the same information and controls.
