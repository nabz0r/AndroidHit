# A readable, reversible adventure — 1.8

Luma Drift 1.8 changes the full default route and teaches it through play. [Rules and content](PROGRESSION_V4.md) · [Tutorial state and reward](TUTORIAL.md) · [Release evidence](RELEASE_1_8.md).

## First visit, guest or Google

A newly initialized local profile enters **Little star school**. Google is optional. Seven short boards teach arrow taps and directional drags, a blocked path, a key/seal pair, opening a closed gate, previewing a switch, refunding Undo, and an inventory-free Spark. The expected object glows and an illustrated fingertip demonstrates the gesture. A tap is an equivalent control. Practice has no failure timer and spends no campaign actions or powers.

**Later** stays visible near the heading. Skipping retains the exact lesson, phase and typed board history; Home and Help resume it. Finishing all lessons reveals a once-per-installation gift of **100 LumaCoins and 2 Sparks**, claimed explicitly. Replaying is free and never grants the gift twice. A completed but unclaimed gift remains reachable without replaying. A failed durable write blocks practice behind Retry; receipt status is cached for rendering rather than flushing a failed transaction every animation frame. Practice does not create campaign wins, mission wins or premium funds.

In a compact landscape window, the practice board and instruction occupy two columns. Long pages scroll with native accessibility scroll actions. The app honors its reduced-motion setting and Android's disabled animator setting. The still version retains the arrow, highlight, instructions and all controls.

## Every new board

The default **L/M** families contain 1,000 Journey and 240 Remix boards. A piece exit and a manual closed-gate opening each cost one action. The initial budget is the certified exact minimum plus a fixed published margin. A different order can require extra openings, so the budget now measures productive planning rather than only N exits and tapping errors.

A switch/gate tap first shows its **immediate** open/closed consequences. Confirm spends one action; cancel spends none. This is not an oracle that rejects every inefficient choice. Ordinary arrows accept tap or a matching drag. Keys and their fixed seals share a numbered identity. The real remaining-piece count and progress meter update after actual moves; the last-three message uses that count.

Undo in policy 3 restores the last productive action and refunds exactly one action, including at zero. It costs no inventory or currency. Current 1.7 policy-2 attempts migrate deterministically, retaining their old geometry and allowance and refunding past Undo overhead. The much older policy-1 active attempt retains its original accounting until restarted; a new attempt uses reversible policy 3. Old challenge codes preserve their old geometry/rules. [Compatibility](PROGRESSION_V4.md).

No new-route state is a logical dead end: opening a required gate remains a valid recovery, although it may need more actions than remain in the current allowance. The free options are Undo or restart. Continuations are optional, capped at two, and quote their exact earned-coin price or rewarded-ad offer. They do not silently change board difficulty, future prices or odds. Real checkout and live advertising remain closed in this candidate.

Exact planning runs on an isolated board snapshot, on one worker with one queued latest request. Generation checks discard stale results. Animation and input do not run exhaustive searches. Powers wait for current guidance before reserving one charge. Spark/Trail/Flow and Focus understand both exits and encoded gate-opening actions. Vision shows legally movable pieces; legality alone does not promise the most efficient completion.

## An inviting home and possessions

Cream and lilac surfaces, dark plum text, soft lower edges and original plush illustrations replace the previous dark panels. A generated Dream Glade surrounds readable surfaces rather than carrying baked-in labels. Home names the signed-in state or explicitly identifies the guest, places the current adventure first, and shows the actual distance to the next earned five-clear gift. No absent day removes a visit reward.

Map, events, inventory and shop remain visible in the bottom navigation. The home card leads to resumed practice or its unclaimed welcome gift. The wardrobe distinguishes equipped, owned and unowned items, includes the acquisition source, and now labels the equipped action consistently. LumaCoins remain local earned funds; LumaGold is the separate premium account wallet. No automatic conversion or fictitious monetary savings is shown.

The six powers have distinct silhouettes: lantern, eye crystal, cloud, shield, glasses and comet. Four wardrobe slots retain their own silhouettes. New clothing is cosmetic only; the older Head/Aura/Charm equipment retains its separately documented bounded effects.

## Rhythm, feedback and motivation

A successful exit has a short departure trail, sound and a real progress increment. Keys and gates use their own feedback. The companion reacts to consecutive successful actions; a correction does not remove saved progression or the daily visit cycle. Milestones, collection totals and progress bars represent actual earned state. Event dates come from the existing calendar; no fabricated countdown, fake near miss or changing goal is added. The ten original synthesized biome arrangements and music/effects controls are retained.

This is a design implementation, not a measurement that players stay longer. The next human checks should observe whether a first-time player can identify the key, predict a switch, recover freely, tell owned from unowned, and explain both currencies. Record confusion, voluntary replay and later complete D1/D7 cohorts before attributing retention to the design. No gameplay analytics SDK or remote behavioral targeting was added.

## Research used, with limits

- [Game Accessibility Guidelines: interactive tutorials](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/include-interactive-tutorials/) supports practicing an action in its gameplay context rather than memorizing a long instruction sheet.
- [Replayable instructions](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/allow-all-narrative-and-instructions-to-be-replayed/) supports returning after interruption or forgetting.
- [Nielsen Norman Group: recognition and recall](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/recognition-and-recall/) supports visible cues and contextual instruction; it does not establish Luma Drift's commercial performance.
- [Material Design: duration and easing](https://m1.material.io/motion/duration-easing.html) informs short responsive transitions. [Android motion settings](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/11183305) informs honoring disabled animation.

Sources consulted 27 October 2026. These are interface/accessibility references, not evidence of a neurological mechanism or a guaranteed hit. Native automated tests, a visual review and real human comprehension remain different evidence.
